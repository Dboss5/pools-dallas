START WITH THE CONDITION OF YOUR POOL

Where should I start?

The useful starting point is your pool’s condition today: its water clarity, debris load, equipment, and how often it is used. A maintained pool and a neglected green pool need different scopes. Confirm what the weekly plan includes before comparing monthly prices.

What does weekly pool service include?

A typical visit combines debris removal, brushing, basket care, water testing, and an equipment check. Vacuuming and chemical adjustments depend on the pool and agreed plan. Filter deep cleaning, salt-cell cleaning, leak detection, and repair work may be separate services. Ask for the inclusions in writing so a short visit is not mistaken for a complete maintenance plan. PoolBurg’s service checklist explains what to ask about.

Why is my pool still cloudy?

Cloudy water can involve filtration, circulation, water balance, or a combination of those problems. Skimming a clean-looking surface does not tell you whether the filter is capturing fine debris or the water chemistry is in range. Share recent water-test results, filter-pressure changes, and whether the returns feel weaker. Persistent poor flow may need equipment diagnosis alongside cleaning.

Why does my pool turn green after rain?

A storm can add debris and runoff while changing water balance and sanitizer demand. The right response starts with testing and circulation checks rather than assuming every green tint needs the same treatment. A pool that has already become green may need a separate recovery service before regular visits begin. This PoolBurg guide to green water after rain covers the distinction in more detail.

Is weekly cleaning enough?

Weekly visits provide a regular maintenance rhythm, but a storm, heavy use, falling leaves, or equipment failure can create work between visits. Ask what you should monitor between appointments, how to report a change, and whether additional visits are available. PoolBurg’s weekly maintenance routine connects cleaning, chemistry, and circulation without treating every pool as identical.

Does a saltwater pool need cleaning?

Yes. A salt chlorine generator does not remove leaves, brush surfaces, empty baskets, or inspect the filtration system. Its output also depends on operating conditions and equipment condition. Tell the team that your pool uses a salt system and ask whether cell inspection or cleaning is part of the plan or quoted separately.

How much does weekly pool service cost?

Pricing depends on pool size, condition, debris, access, service frequency, and the agreed chemical and cleaning scope. Request a quote for your address rather than comparing a monthly headline price alone. Confirm whether an initial cleanup, filter service, or equipment repair is separate, and how extra work will be approved.
