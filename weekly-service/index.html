Weekly Pool Cleaning
in Dallas–DFW.

Weekly pool care is a repeating process of cleaning, testing, checking circulation, and recording what changed. The goal is a plan that fits your pool’s condition and use, with a clear boundary between normal maintenance, deep cleaning, and repair work.

THE WORK BEHIND THE RESULT

What should the service cover?

Remove the debris

Surface skimming, netting, brushing, floor care, and basket attention address different parts of the cleaning load.

Test and assess the water

Review sanitizer, pH, and other balance readings appropriate to the pool and current conditions; record actions rather than judging by color alone.

Check operation and communicate

Observe circulation, filter pressure, visible leaks, and equipment behavior, then document work and recommendations.

What should happen during a weekly visit?

PoolBurg’s published weekly scope covers water testing and balancing, cleaning the floor and surfaces, basket care, equipment observations, filter and salt-system attention, and service notes. The actual work on a particular visit depends on the pool and agreed plan. Ask how deep cleaning, specialty treatments, and parts are distinguished from recurring work. The PoolBurg service checklist gives a useful comparison.

Why do brushing and vacuuming both matter?

They address different places where material collects. Brushing reaches walls, steps, benches, and other surfaces, while floor cleaning removes settled debris. A cleaner moving across the floor does not establish that every surface has been brushed. Explain which features your pool has, including a spa or shallow ledge, when confirming the cleaning plan.

Which water readings should be recorded?

Discuss sanitizer, pH, alkalinity, stabilizer, and calcium hardness as appropriate to the pool and current conditions. The visit record should identify what was measured, any adjustment made, and what needs attention afterward. Clear water is only one observation. The CDC’s home-pool testing guidance explains why sanitizer and pH checks also matter between service visits.

Do I need to test water between visits?

A weekly appointment does not mean the water remains unchanged until the next one. Swimming, weather, debris, and equipment operation can affect the pool between visits. Ask the team for an owner routine that follows your equipment and product instructions, including how to report abnormal readings. The CDC recommends regular testing more frequently than a weekly visit, particularly when use increases.

What if my pool uses a salt system?

Salt equipment produces chlorine; it does not replace physical cleaning, water testing, or filtration. Confirm how the plan handles the cell and operating readings, then use the saltwater maintenance guide for warning lights, scale, and questions about cleaning or diagnosis.

When is filter deep cleaning separate?

Filter condition and model determine the work. A routine check or model-appropriate attention during a visit can differ from disassembly, deep cleaning, inspection, and replacement elements. The filter-cleaning guide explains what to confirm when pressure, flow, or repeated cloudiness suggests a dedicated appointment.

Why does the pool look worse after a storm?

A storm can change debris load, water condition, and equipment operation. Tell the team about runoff, overflow, power interruptions, and visible changes instead of waiting for the usual visit without reporting the issue. If the pool has become green or heavily clouded, recovery work may need a different scope from ongoing care.

Can I start weekly care with a dirty pool?

Yes, request an assessment, but clarify the starting work. A pool with accumulated debris, green water, or a circulation fault may need a cleanup or repair before its normal routine is established. Compare one-time cleaning with recurring care so the first visit and ongoing price describe different tasks clearly.

How much does weekly pool cleaning cost?

Pool size, features, condition, debris, access, equipment, and the agreed service cadence influence the quote. Ask about standard chemical inclusions, specialty treatments, deep cleaning, and repair charges. Compare written scopes rather than the headline price alone. PoolBurg’s maintenance-routine guide connects the ongoing tasks to the pool’s needs.

What should my visit notes tell me?

A useful record explains the work completed, water readings and adjustments, equipment observations, anything that prevented normal service, and recommended next steps. Ask which findings require your approval, how to contact the team between visits, and what instructions apply after a treatment or change. The notes should help you understand the visit without claiming that one reading guarantees the entire week.

What should I send before booking?

Photos, equipment labels, recent service history, and the actual property address help the team establish the starting condition. Tell the team about a preferred date, gate access, or pets when requesting a visit. Confirm scheduling, the initial scope, and pricing directly.

01.

What needs attention first?

02.

What work is included?
