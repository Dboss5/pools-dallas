THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

The Denton County portion is part of Frisco’s two-county geography. This page is for Frisco properties, not an offer of service across all of Denton County. Confirm the actual address and jurisdiction before planning work: a mailing description or nearby landmark is not enough. The practical focus here is water movement, refill activity, cleanup scope, and the property details that affect a pool-care visit.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Denton County side of Frisco. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

Why am I adding water every few days?

Start a dated record of the level, refill or autofill activity, pump schedule, water-feature use, and visible wet areas. Weather and swimming affect water use, but a change from the normal pattern deserves investigation. Ask what tests will help distinguish expected loss from a leak before authorizing repairs or assuming that the same amount is normal for every pool.

Explore the scope: Pool Leak Detection & Repair

Could wet landscaping come from the pool?

A damp area may involve irrigation, recent rain, drainage, fill plumbing, or the pool system. Photograph its position relative to equipment and note when it appears. Tell the team if the pattern changes with pool operation or watering. Testing should establish the source before excavation; a wet patch alone does not locate a buried pool leak.

Explore the scope: Pool Leak Detection & Repair

Can green water be fixed without draining?

The answer depends on the water, accumulated material, finish, and whether the equipment can support a recovery plan. Begin with assessment rather than emptying the pool. Ask what cleanup, testing, filtration, and follow-up are proposed and what would change that plan. A private pool near open space does not automatically need the same treatment as another property.

Explore the scope: Green Pool Cleanup

Where can drained pool water go?

Discharge needs a site-specific plan and current local guidance, particularly for a saltwater pool. The City of Frisco publishes pool-maintenance guidance through its stormwater resources. Have the provider confirm the appropriate method and responsibilities before work begins. Do not direct chemically treated water toward a neighbor, shared landscape, or drain on the assumption that county location makes it acceptable.

Explore the scope: Pool Acid Washing & Stain Care

Does fill-water quality explain the scale?

A drinking-water report offers context, but the pool’s own testing and history are needed to assess scale or recurring deposits. Frequent refills, treatment history, surfaces, and operating conditions belong in the discussion. County lines do not establish a single hardness value for every address. Ask what was measured before deciding on a waterline treatment or equipment cleaning.

Explore the scope: Pool Tile & Waterline Cleaning

Is salt-cell care part of a weekly visit?

Ask whether the plan includes inspecting the system, interpreting alerts, and cleaning the cell when appropriate. Those are distinct from ordinary debris removal and water testing. Bring the model number and service history so the scope follows the equipment’s requirements. Confirm filter deep cleaning and repairs separately, particularly if a recent cleanup has changed the workload.

Explore the scope: Saltwater Pool Maintenance

What affects a Frisco service appointment?

The address, access route, backyard gate, pets, equipment location, and current pool condition all matter. A neglected pool, an active leak concern, and routine weekly care should not be booked as identical work. Describe constraints before agreeing on a visit and ask how unexpected conditions or additional repairs will be approved. Availability is confirmed directly rather than promised by the county label.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Confirm the address, refill pattern, and visible water movement.
  2. Scope cleanup or leak testing before selecting a cosmetic treatment.
  3. Plan discharge, refill, access, and follow-up responsibilities when relevant.

What should I have ready?

  • Refill and autofill notes with dates
  • Wet-area photos and irrigation timing
  • Pool and salt-system details, if installed
  • Drainage or access constraints relevant to the work

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
