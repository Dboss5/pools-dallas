START WITH WHAT YOU NOTICE

What is your pool
trying to tell you?

A symptom is a useful starting point. These common questions help you find the relevant service guide.

Why is my pool still cloudy?

Cloudiness can involve water balance, filtration, or circulation. If the haze returns after cleaning, share recent test results and changes in flow. Compare weekly cleaning and water care with an equipment assessment rather than treating every cloudy pool the same way.

Why am I always adding water?

Weather, pool use, and leaks can all affect the water level. Record what has changed, including autofill behavior and visible wet areas. The pool leak detection guide explains the observations and testing scope to discuss before choosing a repair.

Does my pool pump need replacing?

A leaking seal, a control problem, and a failed motor do not require identical work. Start with pool equipment diagnosis, the model, and the actual fault. Our pool equipment repair guide explains why a puddle alone is not a replacement verdict.

Can stains be cleaned without resurfacing?

A sound but stained finish may suit a cleaning treatment; a worn or separating finish needs a different assessment. Compare stain and surface care with renovation. For marks that keep coming back, our surface and stain care guide explains why the source matters.

BEFORE YOU REQUEST SERVICE

Pool service questions,
answered.

How much does pool service in Frisco cost?

The quote depends on the work: recurring maintenance, an initial cleanup, equipment diagnosis, leak testing, or renovation. Pool size, condition, equipment, and access also matter. Ask for the inclusions, any diagnostic fee, and the process for approving additional work. A service-specific guide can help you compare the scope.

What should weekly pool cleaning include?

Discuss debris removal, brushing, basket care, water testing, and equipment observations. Vacuuming, chemicals, filter deep cleaning, and salt-cell care depend on the agreed plan. The weekly service page explains what to confirm; our weekly pool care guide adds detail about routine tasks and separate work.

Can you clean a pool that has turned green?

Describe its current condition before booking regular care. A green or neglected pool may need an initial assessment and recovery scope, including water testing and circulation checks. It should not automatically receive the same plan as an already maintained pool.

Do you service saltwater pools?

Salt systems still need cleaning, water testing, and circulation checks. Tell the team which salt system you have and ask whether cell inspection or cleaning is included or quoted separately. The chlorine generator does not replace the rest of a maintenance routine.

Can you help when my pool heater stops working?

Share the heater type, model, displayed error, and whether the issue affects the pool, spa, or both. Flow, settings, controls, and equipment condition can affect heating. The heating and cooling guide explains what to discuss for a repair or comfort upgrade.

Can repairs be planned with a pool remodel?

Yes. Discuss known leaks, equipment concerns, and damaged finishes together so necessary repairs can be considered before new work covers them. The pool remodeling guide helps separate finish work, equipment changes, and a broader renovation scope.

Use the service request link or call 945-777-3345. Share the property address, what you have noticed, photos, and equipment details if available. The homepage planner saves a brief for you; appointment availability and the service scope are confirmed directly with Frisco Pool Services.
