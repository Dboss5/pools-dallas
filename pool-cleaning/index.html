THE WORK BEHIND THE RESULT

What should the service cover?

Cleaning

Remove floating and settled debris, brush surfaces, and care for collection points that affect flow.

Circulation

Look at pump operation, filters, returns, and the conditions that move debris through the system.

Water care

Use testing and records to guide adjustments, then review changes caused by use, weather, or equipment.

Which pool cleaning service do I need?

Start with the condition of the water and whether equipment works. An otherwise clear pool with accumulated leaves may suit one-time cleaning. Repeating upkeep fits weekly maintenance. Green water needs a recovery assessment, while poor flow or recurring pressure changes may need filter care or repair alongside cleaning.

Why does the pool stay dirty after cleaning?

Look at where the material is coming from and how it is being removed. Nearby debris, a cleaner that misses surfaces, ineffective filtration, and weak circulation can create different recurring patterns. Describe what returns, how quickly, and whether the equipment behaves differently. The answer may be a change in routine or an equipment assessment rather than simply a longer skim.

What is included in pool maintenance?

PoolBurg’s published weekly scope brings physical cleaning, water testing, basket and filter attention, equipment observations, and visit notes together. Plans should also clarify deep cleaning, specialty treatments, and repairs. PoolBurg’s service checklist helps distinguish the regular work from separately scoped tasks.

Can cleaning fix cloudy water?

Cleaning may form part of the response, but cloudiness can involve water balance, fine particles, filtration, and circulation. A clean-looking surface does not prove the rest of the system works. Share recent water tests, cleaning history, and changes in return flow. The filter guide explains when a deeper equipment check belongs in the conversation.

Does saltwater make maintenance easier?

Salt equipment changes how chlorine is supplied, not the need to remove debris or test the water. The cell, water balance, and flow conditions still need attention. Explore saltwater maintenance before assuming a warning light means that the cell needs replacing.

Is waterline cleaning the same as surface restoration?

No. A waterline film, hard scale, rust-colored spot, and failing finish need different assessments. Start with tile and waterline cleaning for localized marks, or compare stain and surface care when the wider finish is affected. Cleaning does not rebuild damaged material.

How do I compare pool-cleaning quotes?

Compare the same starting condition and work. Ask about physical tasks, water testing, chemical inclusions, filter deep cleaning, salt-cell care, visit notes, and how extra work is approved. A green-pool recovery quote and a regular weekly price may describe fundamentally different work. PoolBurg’s maintenance-routine article helps organize those questions.

What should I send before booking?

Photos, equipment labels, recent service history, and the actual property address help the team establish the starting condition. Tell the team about a preferred date, gate access, or pets when requesting a visit. Confirm scheduling, the initial scope, and pricing directly.
