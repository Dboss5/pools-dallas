THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

Richwoods’ developer describes a private, gated Frisco community with parks and shared amenities. This guide is for the pool at your home, not the association’s amenity pool. If your backyard uses a salt chlorine generator or programmed controls, write down the actual models and settings. The right service conversation connects cleaning, water testing, flow, and the control system rather than treating a warning light as a complete diagnosis.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Richwoods. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

Why is the salt-cell light flashing?

An alert can refer to inspection, flow, salt level, temperature, or another model-specific condition. Record the exact message, model, and time it appears. Do not assume that cleaning or replacing the cell is the only answer. Have the provider interpret the alert alongside water testing and operating conditions, using the instructions for the actual equipment.

Why is my saltwater pool turning green?

A salt system does not remove debris or eliminate the need for testing and filtration. Note the recent generator output, pump schedule, alerts, water appearance, and any interruption to equipment operation. A green-water assessment should examine the whole maintenance and circulation picture. Repeatedly increasing output without understanding the cause can leave the original problem unresolved.

Explore the scope: Green Pool Cleanup

Does a salt cell need cleaning every visit?

Cell inspection and cell cleaning are different tasks. Ask how the model’s guidance and observed buildup determine when cleaning is needed, and whether the service plan includes that work. Unnecessary aggressive cleaning can be inappropriate for the equipment. Share past service dates and recurring alerts so the team can distinguish a maintenance need from a performance problem.

Explore the scope: Saltwater Pool Maintenance

Why did an app change stop circulation?

A schedule or operating-mode change can affect how connected equipment works together. Document what changed and whether the pump, heater, cleaner, or generator behaves differently. Avoid resetting every setting before the visit, since that may remove useful context. Ask for a control and equipment assessment rather than assuming the water only needs additional cleaning.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

Is a low-salt warning always accurate?

A displayed warning needs interpretation using the system model, operating conditions, and a suitable independent water test. It does not justify adding material blindly. Note whether the message began after a refill, a temperature change, or equipment work. The service scope should establish what was measured and what was checked before recommending an adjustment or replacement.

Explore the scope: Saltwater Pool Maintenance

What should weekly saltwater care include?

Confirm physical cleaning, water testing, baskets, equipment observations, and reporting of system alerts. Ask separately about cell inspection, cell cleaning, filter deep cleaning, and parts. The generator is one component of the routine, not a substitute for it. For gated visits, agree on authorized community access and entry to the backyard before scheduling.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Record the alert and current settings without guessing at chemical adjustments.
  2. Compare water tests with flow and generator operating conditions.
  3. Separate routine cleaning, cell service, and any control or equipment repair.

What should I have ready?

  • Generator and pump model numbers
  • Exact alert text and current operating schedule
  • Recent independent water results and refill history
  • Dates of cell cleaning and any recent app changes

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
