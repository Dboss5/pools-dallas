THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

Starwood’s community website describes a gated neighborhood of custom homes on rolling terrain. For a private pool here, the useful starting point is the actual backyard: nearby trees, shade, equipment access, and the way debris moves toward the skimmers. A wooded view does not diagnose a pool fault. It helps frame a maintenance discussion that fits your property instead of a standard visit that overlooks recurring trouble.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Starwood. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

Why do leaves stay on the pool floor?

Surface skimming cannot remove everything that has already sunk. Note whether debris gathers near steps, behind a spa wall, or along a sheltered corner. A cleaner’s route, return direction, basket loading, and cleaning frequency can all matter. Share a wide photo and the cleaner model so the visit can address the missed area, not simply remove the same pile again.

Why is my shaded pool still cloudy?

Shade does not establish the right water balance or prove that filtration is effective. Record whether haze follows a storm, a busy swimming day, or a change in the pump schedule. Bring recent test results and the filter’s normal pressure. Water testing and a circulation check are more useful than assuming every shaded pool needs the same treatment.

Explore the scope: Pool Filter Cleaning

Why does one skimmer collect everything?

Compare the water level, visible basket condition, and surface movement at each opening. An uneven collection pattern may involve wind and return flow, but it can also warrant equipment investigation. Do not adjust hidden valves without knowing their function. Explain which skimmer changed and whether the cleaner or water features run at the same time.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

Can leaves cause poor pump flow?

A heavy debris load can interfere with collection points and circulation. The timing helps: did flow weaken after leaves arrived, or was it already poor? Ask the team to distinguish routine debris removal from a suction or equipment problem. If the pump runs dry, turn it off and arrange service rather than repeatedly trying to restore prime.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

How should gated pool visits be arranged?

Discuss the authorized entry procedure, backyard gate, pets, and the route to the equipment before confirming a visit. Keep access instructions out of public photo reports. A reliable weekly plan also needs agreement on what happens if access is unavailable or weather interrupts the work. Do not assume a community gate arrangement gives access to your private yard.

Explore the scope: Weekly Pool Cleaning

Is a deep cleanup part of weekly care?

A first visit with accumulated leaves and neglected water may need more time and different work from the later maintenance routine. Ask for an initial condition assessment, the recovery tasks, and the point at which recurring service starts. Compare the agreed scope, including filter attention and equipment observations, rather than judging two plans only by the recurring price.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Map the debris pattern around steps, benches, and sheltered water.
  2. Compare baskets, cleaner coverage, and equipment behavior with the normal routine.
  3. Agree on initial cleanup, recurring care, and authorized access separately.

What should I have ready?

  • A wide view showing nearby trees and shaded sections
  • Cleaner model and the areas it misses
  • Normal filter pressure and recent changes in flow
  • Your approved entry process and pet arrangements

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
