THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

Phillips Creek Ranch’s official community information describes green space, lakes, creeks, and trails. Those features establish the setting, not the condition of an individual pool. After a windy or wet period, describe what actually entered your yard and water. A useful service plan separates floating debris, settled dirt, water appearance, and circulation so that an initial cleanup does not become a repeating treatment without explanation.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Phillips Creek Ranch. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

Why did my pool turn green after rain?

Green water can reflect several conditions, and rain timing alone does not identify the cause. Describe the water before the storm, changes in clarity, debris, recent treatment, and whether the equipment stayed on its normal schedule. A recovery assessment should include water testing and circulation. If the pool floor is not visible, keep swimmers out while the condition is assessed.

Can muddy runoff be vacuumed away?

Settled material and continuing entry from the yard need separate attention. Photograph the entry point, deck edges, and low areas before cleaning hides the pattern. Tell the team if the material returns after each rain or irrigation cycle. Agree on an appropriate cleanup method for the pool and filter, and discuss drainage concerns with the relevant property professional.

Why does green water keep coming back?

Repeatedly improving the color without checking the whole system can leave an underlying problem unresolved. Compare the treatment record, filter performance, circulation, and the interval before the change returns. Ask what recovery milestones will be checked before switching to weekly maintenance. Do not assume proximity to a creek or lake explains the condition of your pool.

Explore the scope: Green Pool Cleanup

Why is the filter dirty again so soon?

A recovery cleanup may send more material through the filter than an ordinary maintenance week. Record pressure under comparable operating conditions and note whether return flow changes. Ask when the filter should be inspected during recovery and which follow-up work is included. A damaged or ineffective component needs a different response from a filter that is simply collecting debris.

Explore the scope: Pool Filter Cleaning

Should I drain the pool to clear it?

Do not empty a pool as preparation for a cleaning appointment. The finish, construction, site conditions, groundwater, and proposed work all affect whether draining is suitable. First identify what testing and treatment may accomplish without that step. If draining is proposed, have the provider plan discharge and refill responsibilities using current City of Frisco guidance.

Explore the scope: Pool Acid Washing & Stain Care

What should happen after the cleanup?

Ask for a clear handoff: the observed condition, work completed, remaining equipment concerns, water-testing follow-up, and the proposed routine. A green-pool recovery and regular weekly care are different scopes. If dirt keeps entering from outside the pool, discuss that source separately so the next storm does not simply recreate the original request.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Record water appearance and the visible route of incoming debris.
  2. Scope cleanup and filter follow-up around the actual condition.
  3. Identify remaining equipment or property concerns before recurring care begins.

What should I have ready?

  • Photos before cleanup, including the yard-to-deck boundary
  • When the storm occurred and when color changed
  • Recent test results and chemical-treatment history
  • Equipment runtime and any outage or loss of flow

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
