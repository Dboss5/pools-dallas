Can an autofill hide a pool leak?

An autofill can maintain the visible water level while additional water is being used. Note changes in its activity, water use, wet areas, and equipment behavior. Tell the provider how the fill system is configured; do not alter unfamiliar controls without guidance. Leak investigation uses observations and testing, not simply whether the waterline looks normal.

What should be checked while I am away?

Agree on the visit scope, access, water and equipment observations, and how findings will reach you. Confirm what the provider can do if an unexpected problem requires approval. Weekly visits are not continuous monitoring, and a control app does not replace site checks. Leave a responsible contact arrangement without putting private access details in public reports.

Why is the pool using more water?

Record refill activity alongside weather, swimming, water-feature use, and visible wet patches. A changed pattern deserves attention, but a meter change alone does not establish that the pool is the source. Ask what observations will separate evaporation, splash-out, fill-system behavior, and a suspected leak before agreeing to an invasive repair.

Explore the scope: Pool Leak Detection & Repair

Should I turn the pump off for a trip?

A travel plan should maintain the circulation and treatment the pool needs. Do not shut down a functioning system for an extended absence simply because nobody will swim. Have the provider review the operating routine for the equipment and conditions, and agree on handling power interruptions or faults. A pump running dry needs prompt attention.

Explore the scope: Weekly Pool Cleaning

Why is the spa slow to heat?

Explain the heater type, starting temperature, requested temperature, mode, and any error message. Heating performance depends on the installation and conditions, so compare actual behavior with the equipment’s intended operation. Ask whether flow, settings, and heater condition should be assessed together before discussing a larger heater or a different heating system.

Explore the scope: Pool Heating & Cooling

Can weekly visits prevent every repair?

Routine care creates opportunities to observe changes and address maintenance needs, but it is not a guarantee against equipment failure or leaks. Ask what is checked and reported, what is excluded, and how additional work is approved. A plan that clearly separates maintenance, diagnostics, and repairs makes it easier to respond when a new problem appears.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Document the fill system, equipment routine, and normal water level.
  2. Agree on visit notes, authorized access, and an approval contact.
  3. Investigate changing water use or equipment behavior before planning repairs.

What should I have ready?

  • Normal refill activity and recent water-use changes
  • Autofill information and visible wet areas
  • Travel dates relevant to service access
  • Heating goals and equipment alerts

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
