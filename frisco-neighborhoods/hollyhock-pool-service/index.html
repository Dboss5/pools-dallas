THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

Landon Homes’ community profile places Hollyhock in north Frisco near Highway 380 and describes parks, walking trails, and shared amenities. For homeowners with a private backyard pool, moving in or taking over maintenance is a good time to establish a baseline. A newer-looking system can still need clear operating instructions. Start with an equipment inventory and service history rather than carrying over settings whose purpose is unknown.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Hollyhock. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

What should I check after buying a pool home?

Collect the equipment manuals, model numbers, service records, and any available warranty information. Ask how to identify normal operating modes, the routine maintenance needs, and known unresolved concerns. An initial assessment should distinguish orientation from repair. The age of the house does not establish the age or condition of every pool component.

Why does the pump have so many schedules?

Different programs may serve circulation, cleaning, heating, a spa, or water features. First document which equipment runs in each mode and what you need the pool to do. There is no single runtime that fits every installation. Ask for a review of the system and its requirements before deleting programs or copying settings from another pool.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

Can I keep the previous owner’s routine?

Use the old records as context, not proof that the same plan fits your household. Swimming use, nearby landscaping, equipment condition, and maintenance expectations can differ. Agree on the tasks, reporting, and separate specialty work in a weekly plan. If the water or equipment already has a problem, scope that initial work before the recurring routine.

Explore the scope: Weekly Pool Cleaning

Why does heating work in only one mode?

Record whether the symptom occurs in pool mode, spa mode, or both, along with the requested temperature and displayed error. Controls, valves, flow, and heating equipment may need to be considered together. A useful diagnosis checks the mode that actually fails. Do not assume a new heater is required solely because one screen shows an unexpected temperature.

Explore the scope: Pool Heating & Cooling

Who handles a possible warranty repair?

Confirm the installer, manufacturer, equipment serial number, and the applicable warranty terms before authorizing replacement work. An ordinary service appointment does not establish warranty coverage or make us the original installer. Ask whether diagnosis, labor, parts, and claim coordination are separate, and keep records of the symptom and previous work for the responsible provider.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

Does the community pool share my service plan?

No private-pool request automatically covers an HOA amenity. Shared facilities have their own operator, access arrangements, and requirements. If your concern is a community pool, contact the association or its operator. For your own backyard, provide the property address and equipment details so the requested scope is tied to the correct pool.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Inventory equipment and identify the normal pool, spa, and feature modes.
  2. Establish water, cleaning, and operating records for the handoff.
  3. Agree on routine care and direct possible warranty work to the appropriate provider.

What should I have ready?

  • Equipment labels, manuals, and available service records
  • The operating modes you want to use
  • Any installer or warranty paperwork
  • Your swimming routine and planned changes to the backyard

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
