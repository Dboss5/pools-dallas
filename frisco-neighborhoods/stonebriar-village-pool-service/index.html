THE PROPERTY SHAPES THE PLAN

What matters at your pool?

The Stonebriar Village association describes a guarded community around a golf course in southwestern Frisco, with greenbelts and ponds. The pool in your yard is its own system. If it includes a raised spa, spillover, heater, or multiple scheduled features, explain which mode was running when the symptom appeared. This guide focuses on separating a control or circulation issue from a suspected leak before choosing a repair.

These are inspection and planning questions, not a claim that a particular fault affects every home in Stonebriar Village. The pool’s condition, equipment, and service history determine the work.

Why does my spa empty overnight?

A falling spa level can involve how water moves between the spa and pool when equipment stops, as well as an actual leak. Note where the water ends up, whether the pool rises, and which mode was used last. A valve or check-valve assessment may belong alongside leak testing. A photograph alone cannot establish which component is responsible.

Why did the spillover stop working?

Check the operating mode and schedule from the normal user controls, then describe whether other returns still have flow. A changed program, actuator behavior, or circulation restriction can produce a similar visible result. Photograph the controller screen and equipment labels. Avoid forcing actuators or turning unfamiliar valves just to make the feature run again.

Explore the scope: Pool Equipment Repair

Why will the spa not get hot?

Record the requested temperature, displayed water temperature, operating mode, and any fault message. A heater problem may be connected to flow or controls, so buying a replacement heater first can miss the cause. Explain whether pool heating still works and whether the spa reaches temperature slowly or never begins heating at all.

Explore the scope: Pool Heating & Cooling

Is the waterfall causing water loss?

Water features can change evaporation and splash-out, but they do not rule out a leak. Keep a dated record of water level, feature runtime, weather, and autofill activity. Tell the team whether the loss changes with the feature on or off. The comparison guides the inspection scope; it does not locate a leak by itself.

Explore the scope: Pool Leak Detection & Repair

Should controls be replaced during remodeling?

Start with compatibility and the problems you want solved: unreliable mode changes, awkward scheduling, heater coordination, or a planned equipment upgrade. Keep necessary repairs separate from optional automation improvements. Ask how existing pumps, lighting, valves, and heating will be integrated and tested after the work, rather than choosing a controller only for its app.

Explore the scope: Pool Remodeling

What makes the repair quote change?

One failed component and several systems that do not communicate are different jobs. Share equipment model numbers, the exact sequence that triggers trouble, and any previous repairs. Ask whether the quote includes diagnosis, parts, configuration, and checking both pool and spa modes. Confirm gate access and availability directly before arranging work.

FROM OBSERVATION TO AN AGREED SCOPE

What should the visit cover?

  1. Document the pool and spa operating modes before changing equipment.
  2. Investigate water movement, flow, and control response in the affected mode.
  3. Confirm the repair scope and recheck the features included in the work.

What should I have ready?

  • Pool, spa, spillover, and equipment photos
  • Controller model and screenshots of normal settings
  • Dated notes on water loss and autofill activity
  • Heater errors and the mode in which they appear

Can photos help?

Start with the whole pool, the affected area, and readable equipment labels. Choose photos without people. Our on-device checker rejects detected faces and offers preliminary visible water-color observations and inspection prompts; it cannot find hidden faults.
