START WITH THE CONDITION OF YOUR POOL

Where should I start?

A skimmer leak, equipment-pad drip, underground line problem, and surface defect need different investigations. Useful details include how quickly the level changes, whether an autofill is running, and whether you notice wet soil or water around equipment. Those clues guide testing; they do not identify a leak on their own.

Is my pool leaking or evaporating?

Evaporation varies with weather, wind, water temperature, and use, so a single universal daily allowance is not enough to diagnose a leak. Unexplained repeated refills deserve a closer look, especially when the pattern changes. A controlled comparison test can help distinguish water loss from evaporation, but an autofill, rain, splash-out, and different operating conditions can make the result misleading.

Why does the water stop at the skimmer?

A recurring stopping level can help narrow an investigation, but it does not prove a particular fitting is leaking. The skimmer body, its connection to the pool, nearby fittings, or another defect at that elevation may need inspection. PoolBurg’s guide to skimmer problems explains the clues worth documenting before repairs are chosen.

Can a pool leak underground?

Yes. Buried circulation plumbing can lose water without producing an obvious puddle beside the pool. Testing may include isolating appropriate lines, pressure testing, and locating a suspected fault. The approach depends on the plumbing layout and access. Wet ground is a useful observation, but irrigation and drainage can create similar signs.

How does pool leak detection work?

The investigation can combine a visual assessment with dye testing, listening equipment, underwater inspection, or plumbing tests where appropriate. Different methods answer different questions. Ask which parts of the pool and plumbing are included, what findings will be documented, and whether a follow-up location or repair visit may be required.

Does leak detection include the repair?

Confirm the scope before booking. Finding a leak and repairing it can involve different labor, materials, or access work. A localized fitting repair is different from reaching buried plumbing. Request an explanation of the identified fault, proposed repair, and any impact on tile, deck, landscaping, or equipment before approving the work.

How much does pool leak detection cost?

Pool and spa configuration, plumbing complexity, test coverage, access, and additional locating work affect pricing. Ask for the detection scope and any limits in writing. Do not compare a basic inspection with a quote that includes plumbing tests and repair; they may represent different work.
